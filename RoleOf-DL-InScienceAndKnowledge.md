Philsophy of DL: Lots of small, simple things is better than one complex thing (cf documentary if interested: Fast, Cheap, and Out of Control)

DL is fundamentally emperical (as opposed to theoratical). We cannot know how things work from first principles; instead, we tinker and experiment and explor and discover. This is, historically, an extremely radical idea (cf Bertrand Russell, rationalists, logicians, etc..)

The universe is mind-ogginlgy complex.

For nearly all of human civilization, our approach tounderstanding it has been theory --> careful experiments --> more theory.

With DL, we say: forget about theory, it's too complex; let the model learn the complexity without us having to impose or imagine what theat complexity might be or look like.

In other words: we're replacing rigorous, explicit, mathematical, exact theories, with emperical black-box approximations.


There is a principle, a theorem in deep learning, which is called the **universal approximation theorem.** You're gonna learn more about this later in the course, but it's called the Universal Approximation Theorem and it basically says that a sufficiently rich, deep learning model can approximate any mathematical function.

So any possible mathematical function you can ever think of that can ever exist, a sufficiently complex deep learning model will be able to get an arbitrarily good, empirical approximation to that mathematical formula, even if we don't understand exactly what that formula is.
